How to play.

New to the table? Here are the basics of five popular games. Rules vary a little from club to club, and which games each club offers is best confirmed with the club.

SnookerPot reds and colours in turn and score more points than your opponent.
  • Set-up: 15 reds, six colours and one white cue ball.
  • Pot a red (1 point), then choose any colour: yellow 2, green 3, brown 4, blue 5, pink 6, black 7. Potted colours go back on the table.
  • When the reds are gone, pot the colours in order from yellow to black. These stay down.
  • Foul: hitting the wrong ball first or potting the white gives your opponent at least 4 points.

Most points wins the frame.

8-ball poolClear your group of balls, then pot the black 8 to win.
  • After the break, the first player to pot a ball legally takes solids (1–7) or stripes (9–15). The other player takes the rest.
  • Each turn, hit one of your own balls first.
  • Once your group is cleared, pot the 8 in the pocket you call.
  • You lose the frame if you pot the 8 early or pot the white while sinking it.

Pocket-calling and foul rules vary by club, so ask before you play.

9-ball poolAlways hit the lowest-numbered ball first. Pot the 9 to win.
  • Rack balls 1–9 in a diamond with the 1 at the front and the 9 in the middle.
  • On every shot, the white must touch the lowest-numbered ball on the table first.
  • Any ball you pot legally stays down and you keep shooting.
  • Pot the 9 legally, even off the break or in a combination, and you win. A foul gives your opponent the white in hand.

It is quick, so a frame can be over in minutes.

RummyArrange your 13 cards into sequences and sets, then declare.
  • Each player gets 13 cards. On your turn, draw one card from the closed pile or the open pile, then discard one.
  • You need at least two sequences, and at least one must be a pure sequence: 3 or more consecutive cards of one suit, with no joker.
  • The other groups can be sequences or sets (3–4 cards of the same rank in different suits). Jokers can fill gaps.
  • When all 13 cards fit, declare. A wrong declaration costs you points.

This is 13-card Indian rummy. Gin rummy and other versions have different rules.

ShuffleSlide weighted pucks down the table and land them in the scoring zones.
  • Players take turns sliding pucks, usually four each, from one end of the table.
  • Zones score more the closer they are to the far end: 1, 2 and 3 points.
  • A puck hanging over the end edge without falling off scores the most (4).
  • You may knock your opponent's pucks out of the zones. Add up the points once all pucks are played.

Usually first to 15 or 21 points wins. Targets vary by club.
